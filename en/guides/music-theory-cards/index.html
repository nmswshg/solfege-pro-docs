How to Review Music Theory
in Spare Moments

Review notes, intervals, chords, scales, and progressions with notation and sound. Turn a train ride or short wait—when an instrument and notebook are impractical—into a few focused cards.

The short answer: recall first, then reveal the answer

Rereading a theory book can feel familiar without making chord tones or intervals immediately available on a real score. With cards, form an answer in your head first, then tap to check notation, note names, and sound. Research on retrieval practice suggests that this act of recalling can support longer-term retention better than rereading alone[1][2]. These are general learning studies, however, not tests of this specific app.

What the six card decks cover

Single Notes & IntervalsCheck note names on the staff and distances from a minor second through an octave.
Chords & Chord LookupAnswer chord tones from a chord name, or identify a chord from the notes shown.
Scales / ModesReview component notes from a tonic and scale type, connecting staff notation with playback.
Chord ProgressionsCheck scale degrees and harmonic movement with a stable question layout, answer, and sound.

A three-minute routine: three cards, then save only weak points

You do not need a long session. Pick one deck, say or decide the answer, tap to reveal it, and play the sound when useful. Save only cards that caused hesitation to My Cards. Labels organize themes, and review can use one theme or a mix. Free users can save up to 10 cards; PRO removes the saved-card limit. Batch deletion is also available.

Shared and deck-specific settings are separated

Options shared across decks—such as order, randomization, and duplicate prevention—sit under Shared Settings. Interval ranges, chord types, and similar choices live under Deck Settings. The screen makes it clear whether a choice applies broadly or only to the current deck. This is a reference tool opened beside the metronome, not a scored training mode, so it does not write to scores or training history.

Open a chosen deck directly from the widget

The Quick Access Home Screen widget can target any of the six decks or My Cards. A tap starts that destination directly instead of stopping at the app's top screen. Opening the widget does not use the free allowance; one card is used only when its answer is revealed, from the same shared allowance as the app. Add the widget by long-pressing the Home Screen, then long-press it and choose Edit Widget to select the destination.

The 200 free cards are a one-time total, not a monthly reset

Free users can reveal 200 answers in total. The allowance does not reset each month, so changing the device clock or using Airplane Mode does not alter the condition. After it is used, PRO provides unlimited cards and the chord dictionary. The amount is designed to let you try several dozen single-note, interval, and chord cards before deciding whether it belongs in your routine.

After reviewing cards, move to ear and voice training

Cards are useful for checking whether you can name the tones in Cmaj7, but they do not score your ability to identify a heard chord or sing an interval accurately. After checking knowledge, continue with interval training, chord ear training, or progression training to connect visual relationships with listening. If you are unsure where to begin, use the 30-second practice diagnostic.

References

  2. Dunlosky, J., et al. (2013). Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. DOI: 10.1177/1529100612453266
